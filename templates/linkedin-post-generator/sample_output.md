### Post 1 — founder_story (Mon)

Last Tuesday I almost shut down our free tier.

47% of users were on free. 0.4% converted. Engineers were spending 1 day/wk on free-tier bugs.

Then I looked at where our 12 biggest customers came from.

9 of 12. All started on free.

Killing it would have killed our top-of-funnel. We doubled down instead — made the free tier *better*, capped it harder, and pushed the upgrade flow into the product, not email.

Free → paid conversion is now 1.1%. ARR is up 38% in 90 days.

The metric I was about to chase (free-tier cost) was real. The decision it pointed to was wrong.

### Post 2 — contrarian (Tue)

"Talk to your users" is bad advice in year 1.

Hear me out.

Year 0–1 founders have ~20 users. Those 20 self-selected for *something*. Talking to them harder won't tell you what the next 200 want — it'll tell you to keep building for the 20.

What worked for us:
- Watch 5 sessions a week (Fullstory, not "user interviews")
- Cold-email 10 people who *didn't* sign up. Ask why not.
- Read every Intercom message but don't reply for 5 min — see what they try first.

Talking to users feels productive. Watching what they actually *do* is the data.

Where do you draw the line?

### Post 3 — how_i_did_it (Wed)

How we got from $0 to $100K MRR with 1 marketer:

1. Picked 1 channel (technical SEO). Said no to 4 others.
2. Wrote 1 article/week — but only on queries with proven buyer intent (used Ahrefs CPC > $5 as a filter).
3. Every article ended with a 30-second tool, not a CTA. Tool used the same API as our product. ~60% try it. ~4% sign up.
4. Repurposed each article: 3 LinkedIn posts, 1 YouTube short, 1 cold email opener for sales.

We did this for 13 months. Boring, repeatable, compounding.

The unsexy answer is almost always picking 1 thing and doing it for longer than feels reasonable.

### Post 4 — micro_data (Thu)

I ran 100 cold emails last month. Results:

- 100 sent
- 22 opens (Apple MPP — take with salt)
- 11 replies
- 4 calls booked
- 1 deal closed ($14K)

What the 4 calls had in common:
- Subject was a specific question, not a pitch ("how are you handling X today?")
- Body was 3 sentences
- I referenced a LinkedIn post they'd written in the last 14 days
- I asked for a reply, not a meeting

The 1 deal: subject was "Your post on observability cost — quick question". Body was 47 words.

Sample size of 100 isn't proof. But the pattern is the pattern.
